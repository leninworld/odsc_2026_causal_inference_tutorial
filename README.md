# odsc_2026_causal_inference_tutorial

# Candy-Store Ad Incrementality with DoubleML

## Overview

This project is an educational, fully synthetic example of estimating
the incremental effect of a hypothetical digital advertising campaign on
candy-store purchase conversion. It uses Double Machine Learning
(DoubleML) to demonstrate causal-effect estimation while distinguishing
observed conversion differences from causal impact.

## Objectives

-   Estimate the Average Treatment Effect (ATE) of ad exposure on
    purchase conversion.
-   Illustrate absolute lift in percentage points and relative lift.
-   Explore Conditional Average Treatment Effects (CATE) by age and
    pre-campaign spending.
-   Demonstrate why exposure overlap, covariate balance, weighting, and
    uncertainty diagnostics matter.

## Methodology

The notebook simulates 600 fictional users: 100 exposed to the
hypothetical ad and 500 unexposed. It generates 90 days of pre-campaign
purchase history and models conversion during a 14-day campaign window.
It compares the full cohort with a randomly downsampled comparison group
of 100 unexposed users.

The analysis uses the DoubleML Interactive Regression Model (IRM) with
an ATE score and three-fold cross-fitting. It then estimates a
linear-basis CATE using age and historical spending, and reviews
propensity-score overlap, probability error, covariate differences,
inverse weights, effective sample size, and influence diagnostics.

## Dataset

All data are generated by the notebook for presentation purposes. No real
customer, purchase, or advertising records are used. The simulated data
include demographics, pre-campaign purchase history, exposure,
conversion, and teaching-only ground-truth fields.

## Generated Files

-   `candy_users_600.csv` --- full synthetic user cohort.
-   `candy_users_balanced_200.csv` --- downsampled cohort with 100 users
    in each exposure arm; equal counts do not imply covariate balance.
-   `candy_purchase_history.csv` --- synthetic monthly purchase history
    with 1,800 rows.
-   `candy_simulation_truth.csv` --- simulation ground truth for
    educational comparison.

## Requirements and Execution

Run the notebook in Google Colab using a Python CPU runtime. The
notebook installs pinned package versions and generates its own data and
CSV outputs; no credentials, Drive mount, or uploaded dataset are
required. Internet access is needed for package installation. If Colab
requests a runtime restart after installation, restart and run the
notebook again.

## Important Limitations

This is a teaching simulation, not evidence of real campaign
performance. Causal interpretation depends on assumptions including no
unmeasured confounding, sufficient treatment overlap, consistency, and
no interference. Downsampling discards information and changes the
target population; equal exposure-group sizes do not remove confounding.
Extreme estimated propensities can produce concentrated weights and wide
uncertainty intervals. Real-world analysis should include robustness and
sensitivity checks and, where feasible, randomized holdout validation.
